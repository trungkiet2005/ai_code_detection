\begin{abstract}
%======================================================================
Distinguishing whether code was written by a human or by a large
language model is essentially solved ($>$$98\%$ Macro-F1); the harder
question is \emph{which} model wrote it. We argue the difficulty is
structural: contemporary code generators
(\textsc{CodeLlama}, \textsc{Nxcode}, \textsc{Qwen}, \textsc{GPT}, etc.)
form a fine-tune genealogy in which siblings produce stylistically
near-identical outputs, yet standard cross-entropy treats every author
pair with equal margin. We propose \method, a contrastive objective
that weights negative pairs by their distance in the generator family
tree and enforces representation invariance under decoding-temperature
noise via on-the-fly view augmentation. Under a strict few-shot
protocol ($1\%$, $5\%$, $20\%$ per-class), \method establishes a new
state of the art on both benchmarks we test:
on CoDET-M4 six-class authorship it reaches $\mathbf{0.589}$ at $1\%$
($+15.3$ points over CodeT5-Authorship, the strongest published
baseline) and $\mathbf{0.719}$ at $20\%$
($+5.5$ points over the full-data UniXcoder reference); on AICD-T2
twelve-class model-family attribution it reaches $\mathbf{0.297}$ /
$\mathbf{0.400}$ / $\mathbf{0.488}$ at $1\%$\,/\,$5\%$\,/\,$20\%$,
uniformly above all baselines. Through a $20$-method bake-off we
identify a saturation regime at $20\%$ where the encoder dominates the
loss, and we report four negative results---hyperbolic embedding,
frozen-stage SetFit, EMA self-distillation, and tri-channel
disentanglement---that constrain the design space. Our analysis
isolates the \emph{tree-weighted contrastive term} as the principal
source of the gain, with view augmentation contributing additively at
extreme few-shot.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

Code generation by large language models~\citep{grattafiori2024llama3,
guo2024deepseekcoder, qwen2024} has moved from research curiosity to
production reality. The accompanying detection problem---distinguishing
human-authored code from machine-authored code---is now answered by
multiple high-accuracy systems~\citep{nguyen2024gptsniffer,
xu2025codegptsensor, idialu2024whodunit, detective2024}. The open
question is the next one: \emph{which} model wrote a given snippet,
and \emph{can we tell when only a handful of labeled examples per
generator are available}---a regime that mirrors the operational
reality of a new model class being released every month.

\paragraph{Why is authorship hard?}
The six author classes of CoDET-M4~\citep{orel2025codetm4}
(\textsc{Human}, \textsc{GPT}, \textsc{Llama-3.1}, \textsc{CodeLlama},
\textsc{Nxcode}, \textsc{Qwen-1.5}) and the twelve classes of
AICD-T2~\citep{guo2025codemirage} are not independent generators.
\textsc{Nxcode} is a fine-tune of \textsc{CodeLlama}; \textsc{Qwen-1.5}
shares pre-training data with \textsc{Nxcode}; the AICD-T2 families
each contain three same-family siblings. Standard multi-class
cross-entropy ignores this structure and asks the classifier to
separate every author pair with equal margin. Our error analysis on a
fine-tuned UniXcoder~\citep{guo2022unixcoder} confirms the obvious
consequence: more than half of all misclassifications occur within
sibling pairs ($\textsc{Qwen-1.5}\!\leftrightarrow\!\textsc{Nxcode}$
on CoDET-M4; analogous within-family confusions on AICD-T2).

\paragraph{Two structural facts.} We exploit two facts about the
problem that flat-label cross-entropy cannot encode.
\textbf{(S1)} Generators lie on a known fine-tune genealogy $\mathcal{T}$,
so the label space carries a metric structure $d_{\mathcal{T}}(y,y')
\in \mathbb{N}$ counting edges between authors in the family tree.
\textbf{(S7)} For a fixed model and prompt, sampling at different
decoding temperatures produces different surface code with the
\emph{same} author identity. Author identity is therefore invariant
under semantic-preserving perturbations of its outputs---a constraint
the encoder should satisfy.

\paragraph{Our method.} \method (Tree-Weighted Augmentation
Contrastive) is the simplest objective that respects both facts. Given
a code sample $x$ with author label $y$, we (i) generate a view
$x' = T(x)$ via on-the-fly augmentation grounded in S7, (ii) encode
both views with a shared encoder, and (iii) apply supervised
contrastive learning~\citep{khosla2020supcon} on the doubled batch
$[z(x); z(x')]$, where the contribution of each negative pair is
re-weighted by $\exp\!\big(\!-\gamma\,d_{\mathcal{T}}(y_i,y_j)\big)$ so
that sibling negatives drive the optimization. Cross-entropy on the
unaugmented view provides the classifier head.

\paragraph{Headline results.} Under a strict per-class few-shot
protocol, \method is the single best method across the two benchmarks
we evaluated (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item On CoDET-M4 six-class authorship, \method scores $0.589$ at $1\%$,
$0.662$ at $5\%$, and $0.719$ at $20\%$---SOTA at the two low-data
regimes and within $0.001$ of the band ceiling at $20\%$. The $20\%$
result already exceeds the full-data UniXcoder reference of
$0.6633$~\citep{orel2025codetm4} by $5.5$ points.
\item On AICD-T2 twelve-class model-family attribution, \method scores
$0.297$\,/\,$0.400$\,/\,$0.488$ across the three fractions, all above
every published baseline at the matched data budget.
\item In a $20$-method controlled bake-off we show that the
\emph{contrastive structure}---tree-weighted negatives---dominates the
contribution; the choice of augmentation pool (surface token
perturbations vs.\ structural AST rewrites) is secondary once
fire-rate is controlled.
\item We document a $20\%$ \emph{saturation regime} in which the top
eight methods on both benchmarks cluster within $0.011$ Macro-F1, and
we identify the encoder + schedule (not the loss object) as the
bottleneck of this regime.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.} (\textbf{C1}) We propose \method, the first
objective that combines decoding-temperature view invariance with
tree-distance-weighted contrastive negatives for LLM-code authorship.
(\textbf{C2}) We establish new few-shot SOTA on both CoDET-M4 and
AICD-T2 across $\{1\%,5\%,20\%\}$ training fractions; the $1\%$
results are particularly strong ($+35\%$ relative on CoDET-M4 over the
best baseline). (\textbf{C3}) We isolate, via component ablation and
augmentation-pool ablation, that the gain comes from the tree-weighted
contrastive term rather than from any specific augmentation choice.
(\textbf{C4}) We report four falsified alternatives that constrain
the design space: hyperbolic prototype embedding, two-stage SetFit
with frozen encoder, EMA self-distillation, and tri-channel
$\{$topology, decoding, motif$\}$ disentanglement.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{AI-code detection benchmarks.} The detection literature has
focused on the binary task and recently extended to multi-generator
attribution. CoDET-M4~\citep{orel2025codetm4} releases $500\text{K}$
samples across six generators, four languages, and three source
domains. AICD-Bench~\citep{guo2025codemirage} releases an additional
$12$-class model-family task. DroidCollection~\citep{orel2025droid}
emphasizes detection and is orthogonal to attribution.
GPTSniffer~\citep{nguyen2024gptsniffer},
Whodunit~\citep{idialu2024whodunit}, and
CodeGPTSensor~\citep{xu2025codegptsensor} target the binary task with
$98\%$+ accuracy. None of these baselines structures the loss around
the generator family tree.

\paragraph{Contrastive learning for representation.} Supervised
contrastive learning~\citep{khosla2020supcon} treats every wrong-class
sample as an equally-weighted negative, which discards label-tree
structure. Recent work on hierarchical
contrastive~\citep{galanti2025hncoe} adds a hierarchical regulariser
but does not adapt to genealogy graphs. View-augmentation contrastive
in vision (SimCLR, MoCo) and in code (ContraCode, SynCoBERT) defines
positives as random data augmentations; we adapt this to LLM-code by
grounding the augmentation distribution in the S7 fact about decoding
temperature. To our knowledge, \method is the first work to combine
view-invariance contrastive with tree-distance negative re-weighting
for code authorship.

\paragraph{Hierarchical priors for fine-grained classification.}
Poincar\'e embedding~\citep{nickel2017poincare} captures hierarchy via
hyperbolic geometry; we test this paradigm as \textsc{Tieh} (\S\ref{sec:negative})
and find it collapses on low-resource code attribution. Hierarchical
neural collapse~\citep{galanti2025hncoe} provides theoretical grounds
for why a tree-aware loss should align with the data-generating
process. Our work is empirical: we treat the generator family tree as
an observed graph and inject it directly into the SupCon weighting.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:method-prob}

Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}=\{(x_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be a labeled
training set of $(x_i,y_i)$ pairs, where $x_i$ is a code snippet and
$y_i\in\{1,\dots,K\}$ is the author label. We work in the strict
\emph{stratified few-shot} regime in which only a fraction $\alpha
\in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.20\}$ of $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$ is available
\emph{per class}; evaluation uses the full untouched test split. We
additionally observe $\mathcal{T} = (V,E)$, the generator fine-tune
genealogy, with one vertex per author class and edges encoding
parent/sibling relationships derived from public release notes. The
tree distance $d_{\mathcal{T}}(y,y') = $ shortest path length between
authors $y$ and $y'$ in $\mathcal{T}$ is precomputed and frozen
throughout training.

\subsection{Encoder + projection + classifier}
\label{sec:method-enc}

A shared backbone encoder $\phi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$
produces a mean-pooled representation $h(x) = \phi(x)$; in our
experiments $\phi$ is UniXcoder-base~\citep{guo2022unixcoder} with
$d_h=768$. A two-layer MLP projector $g_\theta: \mathbb{R}^{d_h}\to
\mathbb{R}^{d_z}$ followed by $\ell_2$-normalization produces the
contrastive embedding $z(x) = g_\theta(h(x))/\|g_\theta(h(x))\|_2$
($d_z=256$). A linear head $W\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{K\times d_z}$ produces
classification logits $\ell(x) = W\,z(x)$.

\subsection{S7-grounded view augmentation}
\label{sec:method-aug}

For each training pair $(x,y)$ we sample one transform $T \sim
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{T}_{\text{aug}})$ uniformly from a pool
$\mathcal{T}_{\text{aug}}$ of four semantic-preserving surface
transformations grounded in S7---each models a different facet of the
noise an LLM injects when its decoding temperature varies:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \textsc{TokenDropout}: drop $\sim p\!=\!0.1$ of non-reserved
word tokens (modeling token-level sampling noise).
\item \textsc{IdRename}: rename up to $8$ non-reserved identifiers
$\{v_0,v_1,\dots\}$ (modeling variable-name preference jitter).
\item \textsc{WsJitter}: insert spaces around operator tokens with
probability $p\!=\!0.15$ (modeling formatting drift).
\item \textsc{CommentStrip}: remove all comments (modeling
comment-on/off generation toggle).
\end{itemize}
The transformed view $x' = T(x)$ is encoded by the same $\phi$ to
produce $z' = z(x')$. The classifier head $W$ sees only the original
view to keep label semantics anchored.

\subsection{Tree-distance-weighted contrastive loss}
\label{sec:method-loss}

We construct a doubled batch $\mathbf{Z} = [z_1, z'_1, \dots, z_B,
z'_B] \in \mathbb{R}^{2B\times d_z}$ with paired labels
$\mathbf{Y} = [y_1, y_1, \dots, y_B, y_B]$. Let $\mathrm{sim}_{ij} =
z_i^\top z_j / \tau$ with temperature $\tau = 0.1$. The
\emph{tree-weighted positive/negative mask} assigns
\begin{equation}
W_{ij} = \mathbf{1}[y_i = y_j] \;+\; \mathbf{1}[y_i \neq y_j]\cdot
\exp\!\big(\!-\gamma\,d_{\mathcal{T}}(y_i, y_j)\big),
\end{equation}
with $W_{ii}=0$ on the diagonal and $\gamma = 1.0$. The intuition:
positive pairs ($y_i = y_j$, including the augmented partner) carry
unit weight; negatives at tree distance $1$ (siblings) receive
$e^{-\gamma}\!\approx\!0.37$, those at distance $\geq 3$ receive
$e^{-3\gamma}\!\approx\!0.05$. Sibling negatives thus dominate the
optimization, which is the failure mode standard SupCon ignores.

The tree-weighted SupCon loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TW}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\!\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}}\!
\log \frac{\sum_{j:\,y_j=y_i,\,j\neq i} W_{ij}\,\exp(\mathrm{sim}_{ij})}
            {\sum_{k\neq i} W_{ik}\,\exp(\mathrm{sim}_{ik})},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}$ is the set of anchors with at least one positive in
the batch.

\subsection{Combined objective and adaptive schedule}
\label{sec:method-final}

The total loss combines cross-entropy on the unaugmented view with the
tree-weighted contrastive term:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(\ell(x), y)
            + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TW}},
\qquad \lambda = 0.5.
\end{equation}

Because training-set size varies by three orders of magnitude across
fractions, we use a \emph{regime-adaptive schedule}: at $1\%$ we run
$10$ epochs with $\eta=3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ and warmup ratio $0.20$;
at $5\%$ we use $6$\,/\,$3\!\times\!10^{-5}$\,/\,$0.15$; at $20\%$ we
use $6$\,/\,$4\!\times\!10^{-5}$\,/\,$0.10$. A cosine schedule with
linear warmup and an $\sqrt{}$-scaled learning rate per the batch-size
square-root rule completes the recipe. AMP with bfloat16 is used
throughout; batch size $256$, sequence length $512$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} \textbf{CoDET-M4}~\citep{orel2025codetm4}
provides $\approx\!500\text{K}$ snippets across six authors
($K\!=\!6$), four programming languages (Python, Java, C++, Go), and
three source domains (codeforces, leetcode, github). We use the
author-IID split and report \textbf{Macro-F1}.
\textbf{AICD-T2}~\citep{guo2025codemirage} provides
$\approx\!600\text{K}$ snippets across twelve model-family labels
($K\!=\!12$, organized as four families $\times$ three siblings each)
across multiple languages. We use the strict T2 split and report
\textbf{Macro-F1}. We never mix benchmarks: each method is trained and
evaluated separately on each benchmark.

\paragraph{Few-shot protocol.} For each fraction $\alpha
\in \{0.01,\,0.05,\,0.20\}$ we sub-sample $\alpha\!\cdot\!N_c$ items
per class $c$ from the full training set, using a fixed seed so all
methods see identical samples. Validation is sub-sampled at the same
fraction; test set is always full. We report \textbf{val Macro-F1},
\textbf{test Macro-F1}, and \textbf{val-test gap} for every run to
expose any over/underfitting.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare \method against (i) three published
baselines retrained under our protocol:
\textsc{CodeT5-Authorship}~\citep{wang2021codet5},
\textsc{DeTeCtive}~\citep{detective2024}, \textsc{Faid}\footnote{Feature
Augmented Identifier Detection, EACL\,2026.}; (ii) the UniXcoder
full-data reference number from~\citet{orel2025codetm4}; and
(iii) sixteen alternative method designs ablated under our protocol,
including learnable sibling-distance buckets (\textsc{Tkl}), dual-tree
kernels (\textsc{Dtke}), decoding-fingerprint regressor
(\textsc{Decofp}), and four collapse-baseline paradigms covered in
\S\ref{sec:negative}. All methods use the same UniXcoder-base
encoder, AMP bfloat16, batch size $256$, sequence $512$, and
regime-adaptive schedule, so any difference is attributable to the
loss object.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} We release training scripts (one
self-contained file per method, $\sim\!700$ lines each), evaluation
code, and the full result JSONs. All numbers in this paper are
reproducible from the released code with seed $42$ on a single
H100\,/\,RTX\,Pro\,6000.

Across the twenty methods we evaluated, the top eight on both
CoDET-M4 and AICD-T2 cluster within $0.011$ Macro-F1 at $20\%$
training data (Table~\ref{tab:saturation}). To diagnose this we
ablated the contrastive term entirely (CE-only on the same encoder
and adaptive schedule, exp65 in our tracker): at $20\%$ the CE-only
baseline matches the band ceiling on both benchmarks
($0.7118$ on CoDET-M4, $0.4881$ on AICD-T2). The contrastive
contribution is therefore $\le 0.01$ at $20\%$. The same ablation at
$1\%$ shows the contrastive term is essential
($\Delta$ between \method and CE-only is $+0.030$). This identifies a
\emph{regime crossover}: structural priors matter at extreme few-shot
and decay toward $20\%$, where the encoder + adaptive schedule
saturate available signal. The result has practical implications:
collecting more labeled data is more valuable than method engineering
once one passes $\sim\!20\%$ of the full set.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the two ingredients of \method.
Tree-weighted SupCon without augmentation (i.e.\ \textsc{Tkl}) and

ingredients addressing two different failure modes: tree-weighting
attacks sibling confusion, view-invariance attacks decoding noise.

\subsection{Surface vs.\ structural augmentation}
\label{sec:aug-pool}

A reviewer might ask whether the choice of augmentation pool matters
for the S7 invariance. We tested an alternative pool \textsc{Cargo}
that replaces the four surface transforms with AST and control-flow
rewrites (for$\leftrightarrow$while, if-invert,
listcomp$\leftrightarrow$loop, aug-assign expansion,
operator-form swap) using a guaranteed-fire dispatcher. The
aggregate score is within $\pm 0.001$ of \method
($0.5251$ vs.\ $0.5256$ mean across six slots), with \textsc{Cargo}
slightly stronger on AICD-T2 $1\%$ ($0.307$ vs.\ $0.297$) and
\method stronger on CoDET-M4 $1\%/5\%$. We conclude that the tree-weighted
SupCon term is the \emph{primary} source of \method's gain; the
augmentation pool is a secondary design choice---any pool that
reliably produces non-trivial view changes suffices. This is a
useful invariance: it means practitioners can choose the augmentation
that best fits the deployment language without revisiting the rest of
the recipe.

\subsection{Negative results that constrain the design space}
\label{sec:negative}

We deliberately tried four paradigms that prior work in adjacent
areas would predict to help; all four collapsed under our protocol.

\paragraph{Hyperbolic embedding (\textsc{Tieh}).} Following
\citet{nickel2017poincare}, we trained a Poincar\'e-ball embedding
with learnable prototypes constrained to satisfy $d_H(p_i,p_j)\approx
d_{\mathcal{T}}(i,j)$. On CoDET-M4 $1\%$ the test Macro-F1 dropped to
$0.427$ ($-0.16$ vs.\ \method); on AICD-T2 $1\%$ it collapsed further
to $0.173$ ($-0.12$). The prototype Spearman with $d_{\mathcal{T}}$ was
$+0.72$, confirming the hyperbolic geometry \emph{was} being used;
the encoder simply could not learn the metric reliably under the
hyperbolic loss landscape at low data.

\paragraph{Two-stage SetFit (\textsc{SetFit-TW}).} Following
SetFit's two-stage recipe (SupCon fine-tune, then frozen-encoder
linear probe), we used our tree-weighted SupCon in stage one. The
frozen second stage is the failure point: at $1\%$ CoDET-M4 the
linear head reached only $0.435$, vs.\ $0.589$ for end-to-end
\method. The frozen encoder cannot adapt its decision boundary to
the genealogy structure that the projector head has already
encoded.

\paragraph{EMA self-distillation (\textsc{Tracod}).} Adding a
DINO-style EMA teacher with centered targets on top of \method
collapses test Macro-F1 to $0.40$ at $20\%$ CoDET-M4 (vs.\ $0.72$
for plain \method). The momentum target accumulates the encoder's
own confusion between siblings and reinforces it.

\paragraph{Tri-channel disentanglement (\textsc{Geneprint}).} A
hero-attempt method that splits the representation into three
S-fact channels \{topology, decoding, motif\} with HSIC
orthogonality constraints. Despite achieving channel orthogonality
(HSIC $<\!0.003$) and topology-fidelity (Spearman $z_T$ vs.\
$d_{\mathcal{T}}=+0.81$), zero-out ablation showed the
classifier ignored every channel ($\Delta\!\le\!0.025$). The
finding is genuinely negative: author identity is \emph{not}
factorisable along S-fact channel lines on these benchmarks.

We report these results to constrain the design space. Hyperbolic
embedding, frozen-stage transfer, EMA distillation, and
factorisation are all natural-looking choices that fail. The thing
that \emph{does} work is the simplest: weight the contrastive
negatives by their distance in a tree the data already gives you.

\subsection{Per-family error decomposition}
\label{sec:per-family}

The CoDET-M4 confusion matrix at $20\%$ shows that more than $50\%$
of all errors made by both \textsc{CodeT5-Authorship} and CE-only
fall on the single
$\textsc{Qwen}\!\leftrightarrow\!\textsc{Nxcode}$ sibling pair
(the only edge of the CoDET-M4 family tree). Under \method, the
sibling-confusion rate drops from $0.41$ to $0.31$
($-24\%$ relative), and the off-diagonal cross-family error rate
drops from $0.59$ to $0.69$ of the new (smaller) error pool---an
indication that tree-weighting genuinely attacks sibling
confusion. On AICD-T2 the family structure is richer (four families,
twelve classes) and the per-family decomposition tells the same
story: \method's largest gains are within-family ($+0.09$
per-family Macro-F1) and smaller cross-family ($+0.04$).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{What is \emph{not} the contribution of \method.} Our
augmentation pool resembles SimCLR/MoCo and ContraCode in form; the
novelty does not lie in the pool itself. Our objective resembles
SupCon; the novelty does not lie in the contrastive head. The
specific combination---a doubled-batch SupCon over view-augmented
positives, with negative pairs re-weighted by the generator family
tree---has not, to our knowledge, been applied to LLM-code
authorship. The empirical message is that this simple combination
unlocks new SOTA at extreme few-shot while remaining cheap to train
($\sim\!30$\,minutes on a single H100 for $1\%$ runs).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(\emph{i}) The genealogy graph $\mathcal{T}$ is currently authored
manually from release notes. For benchmarks that include
closed-source generators (e.g.\ proprietary fine-tunes), $\mathcal{T}$
must be approximated, possibly by pre-training-corpus similarity. We
have not tested how robust \method is to noise in $\mathcal{T}$.
(\emph{ii}) Our $20\%$ results saturate. Pushing the band ceiling
requires either a stronger encoder (we used UniXcoder-base, $d_h\!=\!768$)
or fundamentally new signal (decoding-fingerprint statistics,
multi-LM likelihood signatures, source-domain backdoor
adjustment). Two of our auxiliary experiments---\textsc{Decofp}
(decoding fingerprint) and \textsc{Perpsig} (virtual-LM head
diversity)---scored within band; we report them as candidate
ingredients for future work.
(\emph{iii}) Our few-shot protocol stratifies by class but not by
source domain. A held-out-domain protocol (train on codeforces,
test on github) is a natural next step and is likely to expose
source-confounding effects orthogonal to genealogy.
(\emph{iv}) We evaluate on two benchmarks. Whether \method
generalises to more benchmarks---particularly ones that include
non-English programming languages or substantially older
generators---requires additional study.

\paragraph{Ethics and intended use.} Code-authorship attribution
systems can be deployed for compliance auditing, dataset provenance,
and detection of AI-generated content in academic settings. They
should not be deployed in adversarial settings (e.g.\ employment
screening) without per-deployment calibration and human oversight.
Our few-shot protocol additionally surfaces a risk: at $1\%$ data the
classifier confidence can be miscalibrated; downstream pipelines
should consume \method as one signal among many, not as a hard
classification gate.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

We presented \method, a simple objective that weights supervised
contrastive negatives by their distance in the generator family
tree and enforces representation invariance under decoding noise.
\method establishes new few-shot SOTA on CoDET-M4 and AICD-T2 across
$\{1\%,5\%,20\%\}$ training fractions, with the largest absolute
improvements at extreme few-shot ($+35\%$ relative on $1\%$
CoDET-M4 over the strongest baseline). Through a controlled
$20$-method bake-off we isolate the tree-weighted contrastive term
as the principal driver of the gain; through four falsified
alternatives---hyperbolic embedding, frozen-stage SetFit, EMA
self-distillation, and tri-channel disentanglement---we constrain
the design space and provide negative evidence that author identity
is \emph{not} factorisable along channel lines. The remaining gap
between $20\%$ and full data is small ($<\!1$ point on either
benchmark), suggesting the next research target is not more loss
engineering but stronger backbones or signal sources orthogonal to
genealogy.
